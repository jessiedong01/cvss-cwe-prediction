\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Automatic vulnerability triage.}
\citet{elbaz2020fighting} considered the problem of triaging new vulnerabilities as they are disclosed, and asked which triage decisions an analyst could automate using only the information available at that time.
For predicting the eight base CVSS metrics from vulnerability descriptions represented as bags of words, they found a linear classifier to perform best.
They report both directly predicting CVSS scores, and predicting CVSS vectors from which to compute CVSS scores.
CVSS-BERT~\citep{shahid2021cvssbert} takes a similar approach, training a BERT classifier for each metric, and use the specification's formula to compute CVSS scores from predicted vectors.
\citet{marchiori2025canllms} find that a classifier over strong embeddings outperforms general-purpose LLMs, at least on the three impact-related CVSS metrics.
VLAI~\citep{bonhomme2025vlai} fine-tunes RoBERTa on more than 600,000 vulnerabilities to predict the severity tier.
VLAI was trained on more than 600,000 vulnerabilities including CVEs from 2024 and later years, meaning we cannot fairly compare to this system on our test set.

For CWE prediction, \citet{aota2020automation} predict using random forest classification over bag-of-words features.
More recently, \citet{mosievskiy2026roberta} show that a small (125M parameter) RoBERTa language model fine-tuned on CVE-CWE pairs performs on par with larger LLMs for this task.
\citet{talibzade2025llms} find that for both CWE and severity prediction, TF-IDF features outperform larger LLMs prompted generically about the task, which they hypothesize is due to the highly schematic nature of CVE descriptions.
We find similar results on zero-shot severity prediction in Section~\ref{sec:findings}.

CVE-LLM~\citep{ghosh2025cvellm} fine-tunes on vulnerabilities in medical devices, using a domain ontology for supervision, and \citet{torkamani2025casey} use LLMs to predict CWE and severity as part of a vulnerability triage tool.

\paragraph{LLMs for vulnerability-related tasks.}
Recent work has considered LLMs on a range of vulnerability-related tasks.
VulnWatch~\citep{databricks2025vulnwatch} uses LLMs to prioritize vulnerabilities.
\citet{roytman2025benchmarking} finds that, for predicting likely exploitation (EPSS scores), even the best performing frontier LLMs lag behind state of the art statistical models, while costing orders of magnitude more to query at scale.

Unlike VulnWatch~\citep{databricks2025vulnwatch} and much previous work, we run our models on local hardware and evaluate on vulnerabilities introduced strictly after those in our training data.
