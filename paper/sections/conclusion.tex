\section{Conclusion}
A 3.8B-parameter model fine-tuned for \nLoraTrainHours{} hours predicts every NVD enrichment field more accurately than strong TF-IDF baselines on a full year of later CVEs.
The margins are a few points.
TF-IDF remains a fast baseline that any new method should have to beat, and formula scoring, which costs nothing, is the single change that helps it most.
The two trained systems fail on different CVEs, and either one is right on \nEitherSev\% of severities, which is the most promising lead in these results.
Where the fine-tuned model misses, it misses in ways that follow from the text: a single ambiguous word about privileges, a weakness class whose typical score overrides a limiting clause, or a description too short to carry the information at all.
\textsc{Low} remains poorly predicted by every system.

Several extensions follow directly from these findings.
Oversampling \textsc{Low} or weighting its loss should be tried for the fine-tuned model, since the same change helped TF-IDF.
A combination of the two trained systems, by agreement or by confidence, would test whether the \nEitherSev\% upper bound can be approached.
A second training epoch is cheap and the validation curve suggests it would help.
Larger open models fine-tuned the same way, and an evaluation on CVSS v4.0 vectors, would show whether the results hold beyond the setting studied here.
