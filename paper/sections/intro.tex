Knowing the severity of a vulnerability and the kind of security property it violates allows one to triage the vulnerabilities to which their software is susceptible, and prioritize patches accordingly.
For example, suppose one is interested in whether their software is vulnerable to buffer overflows, as in the figure on the left.
The Common Weakness Enumeration (CWE) assigns each weakness a unique CWE-ID (e.g., CWE-120) and groups related CWEs hierarchically (e.g., buffer overflow weaknesses such as CWE-120 and CWE-787 are children of CWE-119), which one could leverage to answer this question.
Alternatively, suppose that one only wants to patch vulnerabilities that could lead to remote code execution (RCE) from untrusted websites, as in the figure on the right.
The Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS) provides eight components to quantify how a vulnerability is exploited and its impact on Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability (e.g., the Network Attack Vector component in the figure, meaning remotely exploitable), and combines these components into a single base score in [0, 10] and a severity tier to ease triage.\footnote{The eight components and score determine severity: $\le$3.9 \textsc{Low}, $\le$6.9 \textsc{Medium}, $\le$8.9 \textsc{High}, else \textsc{Critical}.}
These enrichments for Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVE)\footnote{\url{https://www.cve.org}} identifiers are assigned by humans at the National Vulnerability Database (NVD) maintained by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST).
However, due to not being able to keep up with the number of new vulnerability disclosures, a CVE backlog arose in early 2024.
As such, as of 15 April 2026, NIST will enrich only CVEs present in CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog or affecting US Federal government or Executive Order (EO) 14028 critical software, and mark others as ``Lowest Priority -- not scheduled for immediate enrichment''~\citep{nist2026nvd}.
Consequently, it is important to study automated CVE enrichment.

In this paper, we pose CVE enrichment as the task of predicting the eight CVSS v3.1\footnote{Common Vulnerability Scoring System version 3.1.} components~\citep{first2019cvss31} shown in Figure~\ref{fig:examples}, as well as the CVSS score, severity tier, and CWE from only a text description of the vulnerability.
We consider CVEs from the last three years\footnote{Of the NVD records that were not rejected, \nUsableShareC\% of 2024 CVEs meet our inclusion criteria, against \nUsableShareB\% for 2023 and \nUsableShareA\% for 2022.} from the National Vulnerability Database, since CVEs recur, so training/test splits should account for time and train on older CVEs to avoid leakage.
Specifically, within a pinned NVD release, we train on the CVEs from 2022 and 2023, and test on the \nTest{} usable\footnote{Usable meaning containing required text description, enrichment ground truths, and having a CVSS v3.x (v3.0 or v3.1) base score.} 2024 CVEs.

\paragraph{CVE enrichment.} Given only the text description of a CVE vulnerability disclosure, recover its CVSS vector and thereby its CVSS score and severity tier, as well as its CWE.

We make the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Temporal benchmark for CVE enrichment.} We release a temporal benchmark for CVE enrichment free from leakage issues, based on a pinned snapshot of the NVD and validated with a reference CVSS calculator implementation. We acknowledge that our choice of ground truth, the NVD's vectors, are themselves the products of fallible human judgment: NVD analysts can and do disagree about correct vectors, reporting organizations can assign different scores to the same vulnerabilities, and NVD labels are revised after the fact. But without consensus there's no dataset, and by pinning a snapshot, at least our reported numbers won't change under us.
  \item \textbf{CVSS/CWE Benchmark.} We evaluate the effectiveness of (1) TF-IDF features with linear classification/regression, (2) a 3.8B-parameter Phi-3.5-mini LLM zero-shot, and (3) Phi-3.5-mini fine-tuned with LoRA at predicting (a) the eight CVSS components and deriving the score/severity from these, or (b) the CVSS score and CWE directly. We find that the fine-tuned Phi-3.5-mini achieves the best severity tier accuracy of the systems we evaluate, \nLoRAfSev\%,\footnote{Bonhomme and Dulaunoy (2025) report over 82\% severity accuracy with RoBERTa, but on a random split whose training data includes 2024 CVEs, so their numbers are not comparable to ours (Appendix~\ref{app:data}).} and CWE accuracy of \nLoRAfCwe\%. It matches the exact CVSS vector for \nLoRAfVec\% of test CVEs and outperforms the TF-IDF logistic regression baseline by \nDiffLRfSev{} to \nDiffLRfVec{} points across tasks ($^\ast$ statistically significantly w/ paired bootstrap resampling, intervals in Appendix~\ref{app:results} do not contain 0). We also find that the fine-tuned model and the TF-IDF baseline complement one another, erring on different CVEs: at least one gets severity correct for \nEitherSev\% of test CVEs. We provide more results and discuss insights, error cases, and implications in Sections~\ref{sec:findings} and~\ref{sec:discussion}.
  \item \textbf{Previous Work.} Earlier works have used bag-of-words representations with linear models to predict CVSS components and score~\citep{elbaz2020fighting}, BERT to predict CVSS components~\citep{shahid2021cvssbert}, RoBERTa to predict tier~\citep{bonhomme2025vlai}, and bag-of-words with random forests~\citep{aota2020automation} or RoBERTa~\citep{mosievskiy2026roberta} to predict CWE. Our benchmark expands this prior work by considering a strict temporal split, more recent LLMs, and joint prediction of CVSS and CWE. We provide more related work in Section~\ref{sec:related}.
  \item \textbf{Reproducible Code \& Artifacts.} We release our configs\footnote{Configs to fine-tune Phi-3.5-mini on CVSS and CWE and run all baselines on our datasets: \url{https://github.com/jessiedong01/cvss-cwe-prediction}.} and per-CVE predictions.\footnote{Predictions from all baselines for all test CVEs in JSON Lines format, where each line is a JSON object mapping field to the model's prediction.} Our code regenerates all numbers in this work given an NVD release; we use release \texttt{v2026.10.05-000016}.\footnote{Mirror hosted by the Fraunhofer Institute for Communication, Information Processing and Ergonomics (Fraunhofer FKIE)~\citep{fkie2026nvdfeeds}.}
\end{itemize}
