\section{Conclusion}
We introduced CVE enrichment, the task of recovering the CVSS vector, score, severity tier, and CWE of a vulnerability from its text description, along with a leakage-free temporal benchmark built on a pinned NVD snapshot and a verified CVSS calculator.
On a full year of held-out CVEs, a 3.8B-parameter model fine-tuned for \nLoraTrainHours{} hours outperforms strong TF-IDF baselines on every field, and computing the score from the predicted vector, which costs nothing, is the single change that helps the classical baseline most.
The two trained systems err on different CVEs, and either one is correct on \nEitherSev\% of severity labels, which we consider the most promising lead in these results.
Where the fine-tuned model fails, it fails in ways that follow from the text: an ambiguous word about privileges, a weakness class whose typical score overrides a limiting clause, or a description too short to carry the information at all.
Altogether, we argue that recovering most of the enrichment the NVD no longer provides is tractable with public data and modest compute, and that the remaining errors are as much a property of the descriptions as of the models.
